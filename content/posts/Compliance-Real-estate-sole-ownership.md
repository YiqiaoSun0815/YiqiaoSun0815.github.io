Would you onboard a sole proprietor buying a Spanish office building?

I was recently approached by a potential client structured as a sole proprietorship.
Their plan is to acquire an office building in EU, with existing tenants and stable rental income. They are also asking for potential credit business.

At first glance,there're lot of unclear point:

1) The legal form vs. the deal size

A sole proprietor taking on a cross-border real estate acquisition is unusual.

There’s no legal separation between the individual and the business.
That raises basic questions:

Is the structure appropriate for this size of transaction?
Who ultimately bears the risk and liability?
Why not a dedicated SPV?

2) Source of wealth

This is not a small purchase.

I would need a clear, traceable story of how the funds are generated:

operating business?
prior asset sales?
external investors?

For a sole proprietor, this often means personal wealth = transaction funding,
which makes transparency and documentation even more critical.

3) The asset itself

“Stable rental income” is easy to say.

After checking our internal manuals,I need to check:

SPA (sale & purchase agreement)
valuation report
tenant list and lease agreements
actual cash flow history

I also need to do a project evaluation,including all assumptions,all possible scenarios etc.

4) Cross-border element

As far as I know,any cross-border deal with huge amount is complicated in:

how the property is held (direct vs SPV)
whether there are local partners
how funds move across borders
How I would approach it

I wouldn’t reject this case immediately.

But I also wouldn’t treat it as “standard onboarding”.

I requested a call with the client for further infos.

My takeaway

Things are not simply wrong,but the whole structure is unclear.

I won't approve it until everything is interpreted both rationally and commercially correct.
